Research

I study the origins of planetary diversity through interior models, exoplanet populations, and orbital dynamics. Read more about my projects below.

What are small planets made of, and how reliably can we tell?

Small planets with the same mass and radius can have very different interiors. Narrowing down the possibilities requires models of how iron, rock, water, and gas behave at planetary pressures and temperatures.

I develop flexible planetary interior models that allow us to vary physical assumptions and quantify their effects on inferred compositions. My work on TRAPPIST-1 f showed that assumptions about mantle mineralogy and core composition can contribute more uncertainty to the inferred water mass fraction than the observational errors in mass and radius.

Current projects and collaborations

  • More realistic mantles. With Allona Vazan, I am developing a model of multicomponent mantles that preserves bulk composition as mineral phases change with depth.
  • Materials beyond Earth’s interior. Collaborations on high-pressure water and carbon-rich planets test how different material properties change a planet’s radius and structure.
  • Composition inference. I work with observers and students to characterize individual planets and multiplanet systems, while testing the sensitivity of those conclusions to interior physics.
  • Comparing models. I contribute to an interior-code intercomparison with Caroline Dorn and colleagues, and to community work connecting material data and planetary models.

Why do planet populations differ in size and composition?

A planet’s neighbors and formation environment help us interpret its observed properties.

In 2024, I linked two patterns revealed by Kepler: the regular orbital spacing of many planetary systems and the division of small planets into super-Earths and sub-Neptunes. I found that less regularly spaced systems (high gap complexity) contain a larger fraction of super-Earths and fewer sub-Neptunes than regularly spaced systems. This trend may hold clues to how planetary systems form and evolve.

Current projects and collaborations

  • Planet sizes and system properties. I study how the distribution of planet radii changes with the arrangement of planets and the properties of their host stars.
  • Galactic chemical evolution. With collaborators, I investigate how changes in the Milky Way’s supply of planet-building elements over time affect rocky-planet compositions and properties.
  • Undetected planets. Student-led work examines how undetected planets affect our descriptions of system architecture, including gap complexity.

What determines the stability of planetary systems?

Closely packed planetary systems can experience orbital crossings on timescales that depend strongly on the planets’ separations. Subsequent close encounters can lead to planetary collisions or ejections, reshaping the system’s architecture.

My first, first-author work showed that inclined systems can avoid collisions for long periods after a close encounter. In later work, I showed that unequal planet masses broaden the distribution of stability times at a given orbital spacing, and that this spread increases at wider separations.

Current projects and collaborations

  • Long-term stability. I run large suites of direct N-body simulations to explore how long compact planetary systems survive.
  • Observed multiplanet systems. My collaborations include work on the resonant chain of K2-138 and constraining planet properties by their dynamical stability.
