University of Wisconsin–Madison

I research the formation and evolution of planets from their interiors to their dynamics through computational techniques. My goal as a researcher is to investigate planets through a lens which integrates astrophysics and geoscience.

My work combines planetary interior models, N-body simulations, and exoplanet demographics. I lead the development of Magrathea and co-coordinate PEARL, connecting planetary models with the material physics that underpins them.

Research

What are small planets made of?

Flexible interior models let us test how assumptions about planetary materials affect the compositions inferred from measured masses and radii.

Why do planet populations differ?

Trends in planet sizes and compositions offer clues to how system architecture and the materials available during formation shape planetary populations.

What sets a planetary system’s stability?

N-body simulations help determine how planet masses, orbital spacing, and inclinations affect the lifetimes of compact planetary systems.

Open tools for planetary science

Software and resources

Magrathea

Planetary interior modeling

I lead the development of Magrathea, a C++ platform for planetary structure and composition inference. Its modular design allows for changing the planet model—swapping equations of state and phase diagrams.

Code Documentation Version 2 paper

PEARL

Archive of planetary material properties

I co-coordinate the Planetary EOS Archive and Reference Library, a collaboration between experimentalists and modelers to improve access to the material data used in planetary interior calculations.
